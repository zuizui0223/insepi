Dear Editors of *Methods in Ecology and Evolution*,

Please consider our manuscript, **“When disagreement should not drive sampling: contradiction-guided development of exploration-guarded ecological sensing,”** as a Standard Article.

Autonomous ecological sensors increasingly use targeted acquisition to reduce storage, power and review costs, but targeted sampling can also distort the observation conditions that are retained. Our manuscript develops a simulation-first method that separates biological-event evidence from observation-process reliability. Rather than merging these roles into one classifier, we maintain epistemically distinct observers and use their contradictions to falsify acquisition assumptions.

A central feature of the work is that the original hypothesis did not survive. An early equal-budget test showed that direct disagreement was not automatically beneficial, and a subsequent one-shot locked validation falsified the stronger claim that a fixed scalar disagreement ranking is robust to event-prevalence shift. We preserved that negative result and changed the policy class rather than tuning the failed score against the same validation generation. The resulting method combines guaranteed non-preferential exploration with separate evidence and observability-risk quotas. It also has simple sampling guarantees: for Q = αU + (1−α)R, total-variation distortion from U contracts by exactly 1−α, every target-supported condition set retains at least α of its target mass, and target-to-acquisition importance ratios are bounded by 1/α.
